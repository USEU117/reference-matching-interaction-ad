The resulting bootstrap means and 98.75% intervals are −0.011 points [−0.224, +0.251] for $I_{Equal}$ and −0.085 [−0.296, +0.177] for $I_{Balanced}$; neither excludes zero, so the coarser stride-eight reading is unchanged at the finer grid. Figure S2 reports the single-condition shared-operation ablations and Figure S3 collects the geometry panels and per-image cases; neither supplies primary-scope validation of the shared operations.

Because the pipeline uses frozen encoders and no target-domain optimizer, a training loss or epoch-convergence curve is not defined. Figure S4 instead examines numerical stability of the stored bootstrap estimator by successively taking prefixes of each 1000-replicate array. Across the ten dataset–contrast series and the displayed prefixes of at least 500 replicates, the largest deviation from the final mean is approximately 0.00013 pixel AP and the largest relative interval-width deviation is 6.8%. At prefixes of at least 200, the latter can still reach 17.1%. These observations support the numerical stability of the reported 1000-replicate summaries on the tested prefixes; they do not establish model convergence or remove sampling uncertainty. They also are not a performance-stability comparison of all external configurations: no common metric-by-perturbation experiment was produced for that purpose, so no cross-method stability curve is reported. The plotted intervals are individual 95% intervals, while the adjusted inferential families remain those specified for the respective primary, validation and confirmation analyses.

#### 4.2.4 Resources and Limits

The synchronized benchmark supplements the historical stage records with a common measurement procedure for all six configurations (Table 20 and Figure S5). All six were measured on the same NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3060 Laptop GPU under PyTorch 2.0.0+cu118 and CUDA 11.8. Its scope is deliberately restricted to the three MPDD bracket categories at seed 0 and $K$ equal to 1 and 4. Each of the six category–budget units has one warm-up and three timed repeats, yielding 144 successful runs including warm-ups and no failures in the final measurement file. The 216 unique query images are visited twice across the two budgets. Reference preparation is included, so dividing the total by 432 would not yield query-only latency. The common hardware and measurement procedure do not make workloads identical: the methods retain their own input resolutions, augmentations, feature paths and reference-bank construction.

For each repeat, recorded stage times are summed over all six units; the table reports the median and observed range of the three resulting totals. Dual-encoder baseline under Joint matching and Dual-encoder baseline under Independent matching require 197.118 and 195.300 seconds, respectively, whereas the tested AnomalyDINO and PatchCore configurations range from 24.190 to 78.863 seconds. Dual-encoder baseline therefore has a substantial encoding cost in this scope; the small Joint–Independent matching timing difference does not support a general speed advantage. ## 5 Discussion

The practical implication is to inspect the source of a fusion gain before enlarging an encoder set. The duplicate control tests whether effective weights alone change the result. A representation replacement at fixed weights tests whether the new descriptor helps. Crossing that replacement with the matching rule tests whether reference selection changes its value. The MPDD and BTAD results show why these questions cannot be collapsed into a single three-branch-versus-two-branch comparison.

The interpretation remains limited in several ways. First, the primary interaction was formulated after earlier project exploration, and the datasets are not untouched confirmation sets. Second, the bootstrap conditions on the observed categories and fixed support manifests; its intervals do not characterize arbitrary future support choices. Third, only one prespecified heterogeneous encoder was added, with a smaller seed and budget scope than the DINOv2-S/14 study. Fourth, cross-seed variation is reported only for MPDD and BTAD, full-pixel intervals are computed only for the two primary DINOv2-S/14 interactions and are provided with the reproduction materials rather than in the main tables, and the synchronized resource benchmark covers one machine and three MPDD categories rather than all datasets. Fifth, Figure S4 diagnoses stored-bootstrap numerical stability rather than training convergence or stability across all methods, and the harmonised comparison is a 36-of-144-unit subset. Sixth, qualitative cases are selected extremes, and their Otsu contours are display choices rather than calibrated defect masks. Seventh, the operations that every construction shares, namely spatial mapping, branch normalization and scoring from concatenated descriptors, are held fixed rather than ablated at the primary scope, so this study does not quantify how much each of them contributes and does not present them as validated modules. Finally, the geometric alignment is deterministic canvas correspondence, not a learned guarantee that receptive fields describe exactly the same object part.

These qualifications leave the central finding conditional but interpretable. BTAD with DINOv2-S/14 separates positive representation value from uncertain matching dependence, while the WideResNet50-2 comparison provides an encoder-specific difference under matched conditions. The study's contribution is this controlled and qualified account. A future learned selector, foreground filter or dynamic fusion module would require its own implementation and evidence; none is implied by the present results.

Recent training-free extensions operate on axes other than the factorized comparison of representation replacement and matching rule studied here: sparse hyperedge aggregation (Hyper-FSAD) [@hyperfsad], iterative memory evolution (ReMem) [@remem] and multi-layer attention reweighting (DuoAD) [@duoad]. They address detector design under their respective reference and inference settings, which is a separate question from whether the value of one representation replacement depends on the normal-reference matching rule. Adding them would complement the controlled attribution reported here, but their reference aggregation, memory update or layer weighting would also change the fixed operators under comparison. A controlled test of whether such mechanisms change the measured interaction therefore requires a separate design; the present study keeps those operations fixed and uses 0 target-trainable parameters.
